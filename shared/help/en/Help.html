Getting started

Hearsay records your microphone and the sound of your calls and transcribes them on your Mac. Nothing leaves your Mac unless you send a transcript to an AI provider for meeting notes. You need a Mac with Apple Silicon and macOS 14 or later.

  1. Choose where files are saved in Settings > Output. The default is the Hearsay folder in your Documents folder.
  2. Open the Models tab and click Download next to a model. whisper-large-v3-turbo (1.61 GB) is recommended; whisper-large-v3-turbo-4bit (463 MB) is the smallest good option. If the model is not the Active model, click Use.
  3. Press Start on the Record tab. When macOS asks, allow access to the microphone and to Screen & System Audio Recording (Hearsay uses only the audio). You can change both later in System Settings > Privacy & Security.
  4. If system audio stays silent after you allow it, quit and reopen Hearsay.

Recording

Use the Record tab to record a meeting and get a transcript.

  1. Choose the Microphone. Leave Also capture system audio on to include the other side of a call.
  2. Choose the Language: Auto detects it, or pick EN, DE, or ES. ZH-TW writes Chinese in Traditional characters, ZH-CN in Simplified characters.
  3. Press Start. The meters show whether the microphone and system audio are picking up sound, and a warning appears after 5 seconds of silence. Pause leaves that time out of the transcript.
  4. New text appears in the Live preview about every 30 seconds.
  5. Press Stop. Hearsay transcribes the whole recording once more for the final transcript. To skip this, click Use live preview instead.

The transcript (SRT) and the recording (WAV) are saved in the output folder, named by the start time, for example 2026-09-28_14-05-00.srt.

  • Auto listens to up to the first 90 seconds of speech. If it can’t tell the language, it uses the Auto mode default language and says so, with a button to transcribe again in another language. If you chose a language but the meeting sounds like another one, a banner offers Transcribe again.
  • Set the Auto mode default language in Settings > General. When Auto hears Chinese, it writes Simplified characters if 简体中文 is the Auto mode default language, otherwise Traditional characters.

Transcribing a file

Use the File tab to transcribe an audio or video file you already have.

  1. Drop the file on the File tab, or click Choose…. Hearsay reads wav, m4a, mp3, aac, aiff, caf, and the audio of mp4 and mov files.
  2. Choose the Language, as on the Record tab.
  3. To stop, click Cancel. Nothing is saved.

The SRT is saved in the output folder, named by the time in the file’s name or, if there is none, the file’s date. The original file is never changed.

Meeting notes

Hearsay can send a transcript to an AI provider and save the meeting notes it writes. Sending shares the whole transcript with that provider.

  1. In Settings > AI, choose a provider under Preset:
    • GitHub Copilot CLI, Claude Code CLI, Codex CLI, and Antigravity CLI use the program installed on your Mac and the login you have in it. Requests count against that subscription’s limits. Its Check button (for example Check Claude Code) shows whether the program is found and logged in.
    • Ollama / LM Studio runs on your Mac.
    • Custom works with any OpenAI-compatible service. Enter its URL, model, and token.
  2. Click Test connection. Under Prompt templates, you can add your own instructions for the notes.
  3. After a transcript is saved, Hearsay asks Send transcript for meeting notes? Choose the Template: and the Notes language:, then click Send. Keep local sends nothing.
  4. Edit the suggested meeting name if you like, then click Save.

History

The History tab lists the meetings in your output folder. Use the buttons on a row, or Control-click it.

  • Open SRT, Open Notes, and Open Transcript open a file; Reveal in Finder shows it in Finder.
  • Generate Notes… creates notes for a saved transcript. Regenerate Notes… replaces them; the old notes go to the Trash when the new ones are saved.
  • Move to Trash… moves all of a meeting’s files to the Trash.

Menu bar and shortcuts

You can control Hearsay without its window. Recording continues when the window is closed.

  • Choose where Hearsay appears with Show Hearsay in: in Settings > Window: Menu bar and Dock, Menu bar only, or Dock only.
  • Click the menu bar icon to see the status and to start, pause, or stop a recording. The icon turns red and shows the elapsed time while recording; turn off Show recording status in the menu bar in Settings > Window to keep it unchanged.
  • Press ⌃⌥⌘R to start or stop and ⌃⌥⌘P to pause or resume, in any app. To change them, go to Settings > General > Shortcuts, click a shortcut, and press a new one.
  • Press ⌘, to open Settings and ⌘? to open this help.

Settings and more

  • Interface language: choose it in Settings > General. Hearsay needs to restart; click Restart Now.
  • Startup: turn on Launch Hearsay at login in the same place.
  • Updates: Check for Updates… in the Hearsay menu offers Install Update, which downloads, verifies, and installs the new version and relaunches Hearsay, or View on GitHub, which opens the release page.
  • Recordings: to delete the WAV after a successful transcription, turn off Keep the recording (WAV) after a successful transcription in Settings > Output.
  • Unfinished recordings: if Hearsay or your Mac stops during a recording, the next launch asks what to do with it. Transcribe creates the transcript, Keep moves the WAV to the output folder, and Delete removes it.

Troubleshooting

  • No model installed — Open the Models tab, download a model, and click Use.
  • No microphone access — Turn on Hearsay in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone.
  • System audio off: permission denied — Turn on Hearsay in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Screen & System Audio Recording, then start a new recording.
  • System audio silent after you allowed it — Quit and reopen Hearsay.
  • System audio stops working after an update — This Hearsay build is not signed with a Developer ID, so macOS forgets the permission. On the Record tab, click Fix…, then turn Hearsay on again in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Screen & System Audio Recording.
  • “No audio from” a device — Check that the device is connected and not muted, or choose another Microphone.
  • Poor sound from AirPods or another Bluetooth headset — While recording, macOS switches them to a low-quality call mode. In a noisy room, use the Mac’s built-in microphone.
  • An AI program is not found — Install it, or enter its path in Settings > AI.
  • An AI program is not logged in — In Terminal, run copilot, claude, codex login, or agy once, then click its Check button in Settings > AI.
  • Can’t find your files — The folder is shown in Settings > Output. In the History tab, click Reveal in Finder.
